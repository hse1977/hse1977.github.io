Personal Protective Equipment

Understanding PPE, its purpose, selection, inspection and correct use.

What is PPE?

Personal Protective Equipment, commonly known as PPE, is equipment or clothing designed to help protect a person from specific workplace hazards.

Important:

PPE should not normally be considered the first choice for controlling a hazard. Where reasonably practicable, hazards should first be eliminated, substituted or controlled using other measures.

Common Types of PPE

🪖 Head Protection Safety helmets can help protect against specified head hazards.
👓 Eye Protection Safety glasses or goggles may protect against specified eye hazards.
👂 Hearing Protection Earplugs or earmuffs may reduce exposure to hazardous noise.
😷 Respiratory Protection Appropriate respiratory protection may be required for certain airborne hazards.
🧤 Hand Protection Gloves should be selected according to the specific hazard and task.
🥾 Foot Protection Safety footwear can provide protection against identified foot hazards.
🦺 Body Protection Protective clothing may be required for specific workplace hazards.
🧗 Fall Protection Appropriate fall-protection systems may be required for work at height.

How Should PPE Be Selected?

PPE should be selected based on the hazards identified during the assessment of the task and workplace.

  • Identify the hazard.
  • Determine the required level of protection.
  • Select PPE appropriate for the task.
  • Make sure the PPE is suitable for the user.
  • Consider compatibility when multiple PPE items are worn together.
  • Follow manufacturer instructions.
  • Provide appropriate training and information.

PPE Inspection

PPE should be inspected according to workplace requirements and manufacturer instructions.

Before use, consider checking:

  • Is the PPE clean?
  • Is there visible damage?
  • Are straps, fasteners and adjustment systems functioning correctly?
  • Has the equipment reached its service life or replacement date?
  • Is it suitable for the intended task?

Common PPE Mistakes

  • Wearing the wrong type of PPE.
  • Using damaged PPE.
  • Not wearing PPE correctly.
  • Failing to inspect PPE.
  • Using PPE beyond its service life.
  • Assuming PPE eliminates the hazard.
  • Not considering compatibility between PPE items.

Remember

PPE is an important part of workplace protection, but it is only one part of a complete risk-control strategy.

Always identify the hazard first and apply the appropriate hierarchy of controls.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question:

Which of the following is generally higher in the hierarchy of controls than PPE?

A. Elimination
B. Wearing gloves
C. Wearing safety glasses
D. Wearing safety shoes

Answer: A — Elimination
