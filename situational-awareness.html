Situational Awareness

Stay aware of your surroundings, recognize potential threats and take action before an incident occurs.

What is Situational Awareness?

Situational awareness is being aware of what is happening around you, including where you are, where you are supposed to be, and whether anyone or anything around you could pose a threat to your health and safety.

In the workplace, actively monitoring your surroundings helps you identify potential threats and changing conditions before they result in an incident. This awareness gives you time to assess the situation, make the right decision, and take appropriate action to protect yourself and others.

Why is Situational Awareness Important?

Workplace conditions can change quickly. People, vehicles, equipment, weather, work activities and other hazards may create new risks during a task.

Maintaining situational awareness helps workers:

  • Recognize hazards and changing conditions.
  • Identify potential threats before they cause harm.
  • Maintain awareness of people and activities around them.
  • React appropriately when conditions change.
  • Make safer decisions while performing their work.
  • Protect themselves and others from potential incidents.

Ways to Improve Situational Awareness

Get into the habit of regularly pausing and making a quick mental assessment of your working environment.

Before and during a task, take a moment to stop and consider the following:

A) Is there a threat?

Ask yourself:

Is there anything around me that poses a threat to my health and safety?

If there is a threat, consider how serious it could be and what the potential consequences might be.

B) Should I stop the work?

Ask yourself:

Is the threat significant enough that I should stop working?

If the conditions are unsafe or you are uncertain about the risk, don't simply continue because the task has already started. Stop and reassess the situation.

C) Can I reduce the threat?

Ask yourself:

Is there anything I can do to safely reduce the threat so that I can continue working safely?

Consider whether the hazard can be eliminated, isolated or otherwise controlled before continuing the task.

If you cannot adequately control the hazard, seek appropriate assistance before proceeding.

Practical Workplace Examples

Example 1 — Mobile Equipment

A worker is walking through a work area while focusing on the task ahead. A forklift begins approaching from another direction.

A worker with good situational awareness is continuously monitoring the surroundings. They notice the moving forklift, maintain a safe distance, and avoid entering its path.

The lesson:

Don't focus only on where you are going. Be aware of what is moving and changing around you.

Example 2 — Changing Work Conditions

A worker is carrying out a routine task when another work activity begins nearby. The new activity introduces additional noise, movement and equipment into the area.

Instead of continuing automatically, the worker pauses and reassesses the surroundings.

  • What has changed?
  • Has a new hazard been introduced?
  • Am I still in a safe position?
  • Do I need to change my working method or location?
The lesson:

A task that was safe a few minutes ago may require reassessment when conditions change.

Example 3 — Working at Height

A worker is preparing to perform a task at an elevated location. Before starting, they look around the work area and notice that people are working below.

The worker recognizes the potential interaction between the two activities and considers appropriate controls before beginning.

The lesson:

Situational awareness includes considering not only the hazards directly in front of you, but also how your work may affect other people.

Example 4 — Poor Housekeeping

A worker is walking toward a work area and notices materials and equipment obstructing part of the normal walkway.

Rather than stepping over the obstruction without thinking, the worker stops and assesses the safest way to proceed. The obstruction is addressed or an appropriate alternative route is used.

The lesson:

Small changes in the workplace can create hazards if they are not noticed and addressed.

Engage Your Mind Before Your Hands

One of the simplest ways to improve situational awareness is to think before you act.

Before reaching for a tool, operating equipment, entering an area, or starting a task, take a moment to understand what is happening around you.

STOP • THINK • LOOK • ASSESS • ACT

Engage your mind before your hands.

A Simple Habit

Make situational awareness part of your normal working routine:

PAUSE Take a moment before starting or continuing the task.
ASSESS Look around. What has changed?
CONTROL Take appropriate action to eliminate or reduce the threat.
ACT Continue only when the task can be performed safely.

Quick Knowledge Check

Question:

You notice that conditions around your work area have changed. What should you do first?

A. Continue because the task has already started.
B. Ignore the change if nobody has been injured.
C. Pause and reassess the situation.
D. Work faster to finish before conditions change again.

Answer: C — Pause and reassess the situation.

Remember

Don't just focus on the task. Stay aware of what is happening around you.

Good situational awareness gives you the opportunity to recognize a potential threat, make a safe decision, and react before an incident occurs.

Engage your mind before your hands.
