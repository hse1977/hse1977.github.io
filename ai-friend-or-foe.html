Artificial Intelligence – Friend or Foe?

Will Artificial Intelligence replace HSE professionals, or become one of their most useful tools?

Is This a Tug of War Between Artificial and Human Intelligence?

Is this the tug of war between Artificial Intelligence and Human Intelligence? I guess this is the question every safety professional is worried about. We all know that the contribution of AI in every business is rapidly increasing — from delivery services and cab driving to safety personnel at the worksite.

What does that mean for Health and Safety professionals? Is AI going to take safety officer jobs? The integration of AI into Occupational Health and Safety professions is promising, particularly in enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of Health and Safety plans.

Nevertheless, the question remains: will AI make Health and Safety professionals jobless? Can AI perform H&S roles as we do? Can AI sense ethics, human values and the judgement that we, as safety professionals, apply?

How Can AI Work in the H&S Profession?

Before getting into the answer, let’s understand how AI can work in the H&S profession.

With its current capabilities, AI can assist significantly in identifying hazards, preparing risk assessments, monitoring workplaces, processing observation reports, understanding and analysing incident causation, and even suggesting corrective actions to prevent recurrence.

That is already a significant part of the work carried out by HSE professionals. The important question is whether these capabilities will replace the professional, or support the professional.

Will AI Replace H&S Professionals?

The answer is no. AI can assist H&S professionals in many ways, but AI cannot replace humans — at least not in this profession.

Here, the question is answered, but without explanation or substantial evidence. So how can we ensure that AI will be a friend and not a foe, and that it will not replace humans?

Below are some of my observations and analysis showing how AI can be beneficial and become a handy friend of humans.

Integrating AI into Daily Work

As an Occupational Health and Safety professional, most of our time is spent developing procedures, physically engaging with site workers, reviewing documentation, and meeting key personnel to discuss Health and Safety issues.

By integrating AI into our daily workflow, we can improve efficiency, reduce the excessive time spent on administrative tasks, and optimise performance by allowing us to focus on our primary responsibilities.

Progressing Workplace Safety

Artificial Intelligence technologies such as drones, sensors and robotics can be useful in various safety applications. These include hazard identification and monitoring, identifying unsafe behaviours, and ensuring implementation and compliance with H&S standards and procedures.

Such applications of AI can help identify potential risks and support the prevention of incidents before they occur.

Data-Driven Insights for Proactive Safety Measures

The integration of AI and machine learning with HSE data management systems can provide analytical insights into safety risks. By analysing patterns in incident reports, workplace audits and near-miss data, AI can help identify underlying causes of safety incidents and suggest areas for improvement.

This analytical approach enables Safety professionals to take proactive measures to mitigate risks, rather than reacting to incidents after they occur.

Artificial Intelligence, with its ability to analyse massive databases, recognise patterns and perform complex calculations, provides substantial benefits in hazard identification, risk evaluation and workplace safety monitoring. It can also enhance reporting procedures.

But for successful implementation, several factors require human professional involvement. Let’s understand how and why developing and implementing an effective H&S system in the workplace is not possible through AI alone.

Involving Moral and Ethical Principles in Occupational Health & Safety Management Systems

Immoral and unethical conduct can adversely affect the safety of workers, the general public and the environment. AI operates within the parameters set by its programming and the data on which it is trained.

Human involvement plays a crucial role in ensuring that decisions are made while considering societal values and regulatory requirements. Without this key human role, AI cannot independently perform the job while fully considering the ethical, legal and social contexts of workplace safety.

AI Application: Eliminating Human Error?

Human error is a major factor in workplace accidents, often stemming from oversight, lack of knowledge, fatigue or misjudgement.

AI can automate routine and monotonous tasks that are prone to human error. AI can significantly reduce human error, but it is not a complete solution.

AI systems themselves can be prone to errors, especially if they are poorly designed, based on biased data or not adequately maintained. Moreover, the implementation of AI and automation can introduce new types of risks and vulnerabilities.

The removal of human oversight entirely could lead to situations where AI systems might not respond adequately to unpredictable circumstances or ethical problems in the workplace.

The evolution of workplace safety with AI will likely be characterised by a partnership between human intelligence and artificial intelligence, leveraging the strengths of each to create safer work environments.

Continual Learning and Adaptation Process

AI systems require ongoing input to stay current with legislation and new data. Human involvement plays a vital role in updating legislative requirements, providing and updating lessons learned, and ensuring that the AI system remains aligned with updated safety standards and practices.

By adopting AI technologies, Safety professionals can enhance safety protocols, improve compliance, and develop a culture of safety that is both proactive and predictive.

The key to successful implementation lies in selecting the right AI tools that integrate seamlessly with existing H&S systems and processes, ensuring that data is used effectively to safeguard employees and operations.

Conclusion

In essence, while AI can augment and enhance the capabilities of H&S professionals, it is unlikely to render humans obsolete.

Instead, the future likely holds a collaborative model where AI and human professionals work in tandem, leveraging the strengths of each to achieve optimal Health and Safety outcomes.

This synergy can lead to more proactive and effective safety management systems, where AI handles data-driven tasks and humans focus on strategic, ethical and complex decision-making processes.
