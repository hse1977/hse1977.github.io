Hazard vs Risk

Understanding the difference between hazards, risks and control measures.

What is a Hazard?

Hazard: A hazard is something that has the potential to cause harm, such as injury, ill health, damage to property or harm to the environment.

Examples of hazards

  • Working at height
  • Electricity
  • Moving machinery
  • Hazardous chemicals
  • Noise
  • Hot surfaces
  • Vehicles and mobile equipment
  • Uneven or slippery surfaces

What is Risk?

Risk: Risk is the combination of the likelihood of an event occurring and the severity of the potential harm or consequence.

In simple terms, risk asks:

  • How likely is something to go wrong?
  • If it happens, how serious could the consequences be?

Hazard vs Risk — Simple Example

Example: Wet Floor

A wet floor is the hazard because it has the potential to cause harm.

The possibility that a person could slip, fall and suffer an injury represents the risk.

Another Example — Working at Height

Hazard: Working at an elevated location.

Potential consequence: A person could fall and suffer serious injury.

Risk: The likelihood and potential severity of the fall occurring.

Risk Concept

Risk = Likelihood × Consequence

This is a commonly used conceptual approach to understanding and evaluating risk. Actual risk assessment methods and scoring systems may differ between organizations and standards.

How Do We Control Risk?

Once hazards have been identified, appropriate controls should be selected to reduce risk.

Hierarchy of Controls
  1. Elimination — Remove the hazard.
  2. Substitution — Replace it with something less hazardous where reasonably practicable.
  3. Engineering Controls — Isolate people from the hazard.
  4. Administrative Controls — Procedures, training, planning and supervision.
  5. PPE — Use appropriate personal protective equipment.

Remember

Ask yourself before starting a task:
  • What hazards are present?
  • What could go wrong?
  • Who could be harmed?
  • What controls are required?
  • Are the controls actually in place?

Quick Knowledge Check

Question: A chemical that can cause burns is an example of:

A. Risk
B. Hazard
C. Consequence
D. Control

Answer: B — Hazard
